<section class="language-reading" lang="en">
<h3>English</h3>

<p dir="ltr">Yahya related to me from Malik from Abu&#x27;z-Zinad from al-Araj from Abu Hurayra that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, said, &quot;When the call to prayer is made Shaytan retreats, passing wind, so that he will not hear the adhan. When the adhan is completed he comes back, until, when the iqama is said, he retreats again. When the iqama is completed, he comes back, until he comes between a man and his self and says, &#x27;Think of such and such, think of such and such,&#x27; which he was not thinking about before, until the man does not know how much he has prayed</p>

<h3>English</h3>

<p dir="auto">Report type: companion report. Attribution: Sahl ibn Sad as-Saidi (companion). The opening saying is attributed to Sahl ibn Sad. The subsequent compiler discussion of adhan and iqama remains commentary where supplied.</p>

<p dir="ltr">Yahya related to me from Malik from Abu Hazim ibn Dinar that Sahl ibn Sad as-Saidi said, &quot;There are two times when the gates of heaven are opened, and few who make supplication have it returned to them unanswered. <details>
<summary>English · notes and commentary</summary>

<p dir="ltr">Malik was asked whether the adhan on the day of jumua was called before the time had come for the prayer and he said, &quot;It is not called until after the sun has passed the meridian.&quot; Malik was asked about doubling the adhan and the iqama, and at what point people had to stand when the iqama for the prayer was called. He said, &quot;I have heard nothing about the adhan and iqama except what I have seen people do. As for the iqama, it is not doubled. That is what the people of knowledge in our region continue to do. As for people standing up when the iqama for the prayer is called, I have not heard of any definite point at which it is begun, and I consider it rather to be according to people&#x27;s (individual) capacity, for some people are heavy and some are light, and they are not able to be as one man .&quot; Malik was asked about a gathering of people who wished to do the prescribed prayer calling the iqama and not the adhan, and he said, &quot;lt is enough for them. The adhan is only obligatory in mosques where the prayer is said in congregation.&quot; Malik was asked about the muadhdhin saying &quot;Peace be upon you&quot; to the imam and calling him to the prayer, and he was asked who was the first person to whom such a greeting was made. He replied, &quot;I have not heard that this greeting occurred in the first community.&quot; Yahya said that Malik was asked whether a muadhdhin who called the people to prayer and then waited to see if anyone would come and no one did, so he said the iqama and did the prayer by himself and then people came after he had finished, should repeat the prayer with them. Malik said, &quot;He does not repeat the prayer, and whoever comes after he has finished should do the prayer by himself.&quot; Yahya said that Malik was asked about a muadhdhin who called the adhan for a group of people, did voluntary prayers, and then the group of people wanted to do the prayer with some one else saying the iqama. He said, &quot;There is no harm in that. His iqama or somebody else&#x27;s are the same.&quot; Yahya said that Malik said, &quot;The subh prayer is still called before dawn. As for the other prayers, we believe that they should only be called after the time has started</p>
